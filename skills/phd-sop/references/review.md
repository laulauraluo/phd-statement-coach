# Independent review (Step 4)

Give this file, `patterns.md`, the draft, the program's exact prompt and limit, and neutral applicant context to a fresh subagent that did not write the draft. The coaching framework below follows the MIT-licensed `statement-coach` skill by mohitagw15856 (https://github.com/mohitagw15856/pm-claude-skills), adapted for PhD research statements.

## Rules for the reviewer

- Feedback only. Do not write paragraphs meant to be pasted into the essay; where new material is needed, ask the question that would surface it.
- Quote the draft's lines verbatim.
- Be direct; no praise padding.
- Do not question facts the applicant has stated; judge how they are used.

## Checks

- **Specificity:** flag every sentence that 200 other applicants could have written.
- **Trajectory:** does the essay connect decisions, or list achievements the CV already shows?
- **Show a scene** for every claimed quality (persistence, care, ethics, curiosity).
- **Fit:** specific to named faculty papers and to what the applicant would add?
- **Voice:** one person, or polished and machine-sounding? Flag tidy triads, filler transitions and stacked adjectives.
- **Prompt coverage:** every required part of the program's prompt answered.
- **The 15 patterns** in `patterns.md`.

## Output format

1. Scores with reasons: Specificity, Trajectory, Fit, Voice (each /10).
2. Prompt coverage, part by part: covered / partial / missing.
3. 15-pattern table: pass / partial / fail, one line each.
4. Line-level feedback on the 8 to 10 most important lines.
5. 5 to 8 material-mining questions for the applicant to answer in their own words.
6. Revision plan in passes, ending with what to cut to meet the limit.
7. The one thing: the single change that most improves the essay.

Keep the report under about 900 words. Write it in the applicant's preferred language.
